How the Mosaic Works
Step 1

The Big Picture — What is The Mosaic?

The Mosaic is a living, generative artwork that turns the Ethereum blockchain into a visual painting — updated in real time, block by block. Think of it as a window into the pulse of the internet's financial layer.

Every second, thousands of transactions are happening across the world — people sending money, trading tokens, creating digital art, or using decentralized apps. The Mosaic turns all of that invisible activity into color, shape, and sound, making it something you can actually see and feel.

💡 No coding knowledge needed. If you can read a painting, you can read The Mosaic.
Step 2

Blocks — The Building Squares

On a blockchain like Ethereum, data is organized into blocks. Roughly every 12 seconds, a validator bundles up a batch of recent transactions and adds a new block to the chain — like adding a page to a ledger.

Calm Block
Few transactions, low fees
Active Block
Many transactions, moderate fees
Whale Block
Massive capital movement detected

In The Mosaic, each square tile = one block. The grid fills from left to right, top to bottom. When the grid is full, it resets and starts again — like a new canvas.

Block Number A sequential ID for each block. Block #21,000,000 comes right after #20,999,999. You can see the latest block number in the header stats.
Grid Fill % How full the current mosaic canvas is. At 100%, a new mosaic cycle begins. Think of it as "how full is this painting?"
Step 3

Transactions — The Brushstrokes Inside Each Tile

Inside each block tile, you'll see smaller colored pixels. Each pixel represents one transaction inside that block. The color of each pixel tells you what type of activity it is:

Plain Transfer

The simplest action on the blockchain. Person A sends ETH or tokens directly to Person B. Like a digital wire transfer. No middleman, no bank.

Example: Sending 0.5 ETH to a friend's wallet.
DeFi Swap (Token Exchange)

A trade executed on a decentralized exchange (DEX) like Uniswap. Instead of a centralized exchange like Coinbase, the swap happens automatically via a smart contract. No account needed.

Example: Swapping USDC for ETH on Uniswap at the current market rate.
NFT Mint

The act of creating a new digital item on the blockchain — like publishing a piece of art, a game item, or a collectible. Once minted, that item has a provable record of creation and ownership.

Example: An artist mints 100 editions of a digital painting. Each edition becomes a unique, ownable token.
Contract Call

An interaction with a smart contract — a self-executing piece of code that lives on the blockchain. When you use a DeFi app, vote in a DAO, or claim a reward, you're making a contract call.

Example: Claiming staking rewards from a liquidity pool, or voting on a governance proposal.
Whale Transaction ✦

A transaction involving a very large amount of money (over $50,000 equivalent). These glow bright white and pulse on the mosaic. They also trigger a deep sub-bass tone in the sound engine — you can literally hear the money move.

Example: A large fund moving $2M worth of ETH between wallets.
Staking

Locking up tokens in a smart contract to earn passive rewards — similar to putting money in a savings account that earns interest. Stakers help secure the network or provide liquidity.

Example: Depositing ETH into a liquid staking protocol like Lido to earn staking yield.
Step 4

Smart Contracts — The Rulebooks

A smart contract is a program that lives on the blockchain and runs automatically when certain conditions are met. Nobody controls it — not even its creator. Once deployed, it just runs according to its coded rules.

📜 Think of it like a vending machine: Put in the right input (tokens + instructions), get the exact output (swapped tokens, minted NFT, unlocked reward) — no human needed in the middle.

In The Mosaic, most of the colorful, complex activity you see — DeFi swaps, NFT mints, staking — is driven by smart contracts. A plain golden-yellow tile usually indicates a direct contract call.

Step 5

Gas & Fees — The Cost of Activity

Every transaction on Ethereum requires a small fee paid in ETH. This fee compensates the validators who process and confirm the transaction. The fee is measured in Gwei — a tiny unit of ETH (1 Gwei = 0.000000001 ETH).

Gas A unit that measures computational effort. Simple transfers require less gas; complex contract interactions require more.
Gas Price (Gwei) How much ETH you're willing to pay per unit of gas. Higher gas price = faster confirmation. Lower gas price = cheaper, but may wait longer.
Average Fee (Avg Fee) The mean gas price across all transactions in the latest block, displayed in the header. A low value means the network is calm; a high value means it's congested and busy.
Low Fees
< 10 Gwei

Network is quiet. A great time to transact cheaply. Tiles appear as dim, muted versions of their color.

High Fees
> 50 Gwei

Network is congested. More competition for block space. Tiles appear bright and saturated.

Step 6

Trends — Reading the Pulse

The header stats aren't just numbers — they're a live read of what the network is collectively doing right now.

Dominant Trend The most common transaction type across recent blocks. If the mosaic is mostly cyan, "Transfers" will be dominant. If it's mostly magenta, DeFi trading is surging.
Gas Trajectory Is the average fee rising (🔺 Climbing), falling (🔻 Easing), or stable (→ Stable)? This tells you whether network congestion is increasing or decreasing.
TX Activity Split The colored bar in the header showing the ratio of Transfers : DeFi Swaps : NFT Mints. A quick visual snapshot of what people are actually doing right now.
Stable When gas fees or a metric isn't moving much in either direction. A stable gas trajectory means the network load is consistent.
Step 7

Track Wallet — Follow Someone in the Painting

Paste any Ethereum wallet address (starting with 0x...) into the tracker bar. The Mosaic will watch every incoming block and highlight any tile that contains a transaction from or to that wallet.

Sent
Orange glow border — this wallet sent funds
Received
Cyan glow border — this wallet received funds

This is useful for tracking your own wallet's on-chain activity, watching a known fund, or simply seeing if your favorite address is actively transacting.

Step 8

Daily Portraits & Archives — History as Art

The Mosaic saves a portrait of each day's blockchain activity. Open Settings & Archives and pick a day from the calendar to replay that day's blocks as an animated painting.

Daily Portrait A complete mosaic generated from all blocks on a specific calendar day. Color, complexity, and density all reflect what happened that day on the blockchain.
Calm / Active / Gas Spike (Mood Dots) Color-coded indicators on each calendar day: calm blue = quiet day, amber = active trading, red = heavy congestion and high fees.
Step 9

Sound Sonification — Hear the Blockchain

The Mosaic can turn every block into a musical note. Each new block triggers a sound based on its hash — a unique fingerprint — and the selected instrument profile. The pitch and tone change with each block.

Acoustic Guitar Uses a Karplus-Strong physical string model — essentially simulating a plucked guitar string in real-time, computed in your browser.
Electronic Drums (808) A classic TR-808 style drum machine: deep sine kick, bandpass snare, crisp hi-hat. Whale blocks trigger earlier in the pattern.
Synth Pad A lush, ambient chord (bass + root + 3rd + 5th) with dual detuned oscillators creating a dreamy chorus effect.
Warm Chimes A bell-like triad with an echo. Whale transactions add a deep resonant sub-bass gong that you can literally feel.
